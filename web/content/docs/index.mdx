Clearance is a licensing desk on GenLayer. A creator publishes a work with a license written in their own words and
up to four priced tiers. Anyone describes how they want to use the work and asks. Several GenLayer validators read
the license against the use, each with their own model, and answer one of four things:

| Answer | What happens |
|---|---|
| **FREE** | The license allows the use without payment. A receipt is issued at once, with any conditions the license sets, such as credit. |
| **PAID** with a tier | The license allows the use only under a paid tier. The requester can buy that tier at the stored price for seven days; the receipt is issued on purchase. |
| **DENIED** | The license clearly rules the use out and no tier covers it. No receipt. The creator may still grant an exception. |
| **UNCLEAR** | The license does not say enough, or the use is too vague. The request goes to the creator, who answers yes, no or a price. |

Every receipt is a public page pinned to the exact license version it was issued under, so a creator who changes
their terms later cannot change what an earlier receipt covers.

<Callout title="Not legal advice">
  Clearance applies the creator's own words and nothing else. It does not decide what copyright law allows, such as
  fair use, and it cannot prove that a creator made what they publish. Every receipt says so.
</Callout>

## Why GenLayer

Every request turns on one question: does this license, as written, allow this use? That is interpretation, and
whoever answers it decides whether a sale happens. If the creator's shop answered, users would suspect it of saying
paid too often; if a platform answered, creators would suspect it of saying free. On GenLayer an answer stands only
when validators with their own models agree on it.

The license and the use are short texts already on chain, so every validator reads identical input. The four
answers are far apart, and validators compare only the verdict and the tier, so agreement is stable. Money moves in
two places only: when a requester buys a tier, and when a creator withdraws.
